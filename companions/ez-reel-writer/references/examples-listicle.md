# Listicle Examples: Real Posts, Real Numbers

> **How to use these examples:** they show structure and the quality bar. SKILL.md's
> rules outrank any wording modeled here; if an example ever demonstrates something the
> rules ban, the rules win. Never reuse these examples' prose in a member's post.
> Computed tables are the exception: correct arithmetic looks the same everywhere.

Six real listicle reels analyzed frame-by-frame. Study the shape each one uses.

---

## Example 1: Data story + opinion punchline (Rebecca Foote, GO coach), 2.35M plays

```
TITLE: History of Mortgage Rates
LIST (12 lines, all at once ~1.5s in):
*1975: 9%
*1980: 14.4%
*1985: 13.5%
*1990: 10.1%
*1995: 7.9%
*2000: 8%
*2005: 5.7%
*2010: 5%
*2015: 3.9%
*2020: 2.7%
*2025: 6.7%
*Current 6.375%
PUNCHLINE (yellow box): People waited in 1985 when rates were 13%. Imagine waiting for 3% again.
```
- Footage: backyard cornhole game: unrelated to topic, real life, gentle motion. 8s.
- Caption: interprets the data + only CTA "📩 Follow me for tips…"
- 5.2 plays per viewer (list unreadable in one pass), 196 comments (punchline = the
  argument starter).

A 30 yr Mortgage = 30 years 😱

- Lesson: the list has a narrative arc, and the kicker is the shortest line.

## Example 3: Maximum compression story-list (Shawn Kaplan), 14.3M views

```
LIST (one black box, 10 lines, no title needed):
Dad buys stock at $250K.
It grows to $12M.
If he sells, he owes tax on an $11.75M gain.
Instead, he puts it in a trust.
Borrows against it.
Borrowing is not income. So no tax.
He lives on loans. Never sells.
He dies holding the asset.
Kids inherit at a $12M basis.
IRS gets $0.
CAPTION (entire): Follow the plan.
```
- Footage: driving shot past a gated estate. Trending audio.
- Every line a complete short sentence; kicker is the shortest line.
- Top comment (252 likes) is an expert correction, provocative simplification drives
  debate, but every line must be defensibly true.

## Cautionary details (from the same dataset)
- A $150K salary row that broke the post's own 25% formula ($3,250 instead of $3,125).
- "West Balm Beach, FL" shipped to 1.15M viewers.
- Both posts still performed: but the comment section catches everything. Math check
  and spell check every line.

# Member input to finished output: two real pairs

These show the target quality from real (tested) member inputs. Study the moves, never
reuse the sentences.

## Pair 1: a formula request becomes a defensible money table

**Member sent:**
> "Build me a listicle table: home price, monthly payment, and salary needed. Use 6.5%
> on a 30 year, 10% down, and assume the payment should be 28% of gross monthly income.
> Do prices from $300K up to $900K in $100K steps. Include taxes and insurance at
> roughly 1.5% of the price per year combined."

**What happened before writing:** down payment under 20% and no mention of mortgage
insurance, so the skill ASKED first. Member answered 0.5% of the loan per year. Then:

**Delivered on-screen text:**
> TITLE: What Salary You Need At Each Price
>
> $300K home / $2,194/mo / $94K salary needed
> $400K home / $2,925/mo / $125K salary needed
> $500K home / $3,657/mo / $157K salary needed
> $600K home / $4,388/mo / $188K salary needed
> $700K home / $5,120/mo / $219K salary needed
> $800K home / $5,851/mo / $251K salary needed
> $900K home / $6,582/mo / $282K salary needed
>
> ASSUMPTIONS (small text under the list):
> 6.5% rate, 30 year fixed, 10% down
> Payment includes taxes and insurance at 1.5% of price per year
> Plus mortgage insurance at 0.5% of the loan per year
> Salary assumes the payment is 28% of gross income
>
> PUNCHLINE: "SEND ME THE PAYMENT, NOT THE PRICE"

**Plus the warning block:**
> ⚠️ BEFORE YOU POST
> These salary numbers assume no HOA dues and no car, credit card, or student loan
> payments. If a buyer has those, the salary needed goes up.

**Why this is the bar:** two independent recomputations found all 14 figures exact. The
assumptions are ON SCREEN so the member can defend the post in the comments. The
punchline is an action line, correct for a utility save.

## Pair 2: rate data becomes debate bait with honest counts

**Member sent:**
> "Average 30-year fixed rates by year: 2015 3.85%, 2016 3.65%, 2017 3.99%, 2018
> 4.54%, 2019 3.94%, 2020 3.11%, 2021 2.96%, 2022 5.34%, 2023 6.81%, 2024 6.72%, 2025
> 6.5%. Today we're at 6.25%. Point I want to make: people keep waiting for 2021 to
> come back and it isn't coming back."

**Delivered (excerpts):**
> TITLE: Average 30-Year Rates Since 2015
> LIST: the 11 years verbatim + "Today: 6.25%"
> PUNCHLINE: Columbus buyers are still waiting for 2021 to come back. It isn't coming
> back.
>
> CAPTION: Eleven years of averages, plus where we sit today. 2.96% in 2021 is the
> lowest number on this list. Every year since 2023 has started with a 6. What number
> are you actually waiting for? 👇
>
> ⚠️ BEFORE YOU POST: I put Columbus in the punchline because this is for your market,
> but the rates you gave me are national averages. Swap the wording if you want it
> strictly local.

**Why this is the bar:** "Eleven years" was counted, not guessed (2015-2025 inclusive).
The streak claim ("every year since 2023 has started with a 6") checks against the
rows. The punchline reuses the member's own opinion in their words. And the one liberty
taken (attaching Columbus) is flagged in the warning block instead of slipped in
silently.
