# X-REEL Format Rules

A news article turned into a BREAKING-style post: a static fake-tweet screenshot held
for ~6 seconds on video, with the full story in the caption. Positions the member as
the market authority their audience follows for news. Real posts and pairs:
`examples-x-reel.md`.

The video build is a single static tweet-mockup image (member's name, handle, verified
badge, the hook text, one news image). Dark mode on black or light mode on white both
work; pick whichever the member's template uses. The script you write has
two jobs: the tweet text (on screen) and the caption (the payoff).

Default audience is Realtors in the member's market; every X-reel must answer "why
does a working agent need to know this today?" If the member set a different audience
in setup, frame the takeaway for them instead.

---

## Output format (always this order)

```
🖼️ TWEET TEXT (paste into the tweet mockup in Canva)

[HOOK LABEL]: [1-2 line hook]

[1 line of stakes or tension, when it sharpens the story]

[Bridge tease line] 👇

📝 CAPTION (paste into the Instagram caption box)

**[emoji] [Section header]:**

[3-6 short lines of facts]

[...4-7 sections total...]

**💡 What this means for [market] Realtors:**

[3-5 lines connecting the news to agent business]

**✅ Bottom line:**

[2-3 lines: one-sentence recap + agent-facing action, question, or challenge]

Character count: [count]

🎬 IMAGE (for the tweet mockup)

[1-2 suggestions for the news image, drawn from the story: the site, the building,
the brand placed on the local landmark]
```

**Missing-fact protocol:** if the article is thin on a key detail (location, timeline,
cost), say what is known and note what has not been announced, inside the caption. Do
not fill gaps, and do not bracket-and-ask (the member cannot supply facts the article
does not contain). A dedicated "what nobody has announced yet" section is the strong
play for rumor-stage stories.

---

## The hook

- **Pick the hook label that fits the story:** BREAKING for major news, JUST IN for
  fresh announcements, REPORT for studies, rankings, data. **If you already wrote an
  X-reel earlier in THIS conversation**, use a different label and a different bridge
  line than that one. With no prior post in the conversation, just pick the best fit.
- 1-2 lines. Lead with the single most dramatic verified fact: a dollar figure, a
  ranking, a reversal, a famous name, a "first ever" the article states.
- Optional stakes line after the hook when it sharpens tension.
- Close with a bridge tease ending in 👇, **varied every post** and matched to the
  story: "Here's where they're building 👇" / "Here's who's behind it 👇" / "Here's the
  full breakdown 👇". Never repeat the previous post's bridge line.

## The caption: HARD RULES

- **Keep the caption under 1,800 characters INCLUDING spaces and emojis.** Instagram's
  hard cap is 2,200, so 1,800 leaves headroom for a miscount and keeps the member clear
  of truncation. Print the CHARACTER count on the last line: `Character count: 1,642`.
- **How to count so you are not wrong.** Language models miscount characters in one pass
  (a test printed 1,318 for a 1,170-character caption). Do it in two steps: count the
  words, multiply by 6.2, then sanity-check against your own read. 1,800 characters is
  roughly 290 words. If the estimate lands between 1,700 and 1,800, cut a few facts
  anyway. When in doubt go shorter, because a caption that fits beats one that truncates.
- Count the caption body including section headers, emojis, and line breaks. If over, cut
  facts; never compress by removing line breaks.
- 4-7 sections when the article supports it. A thin article gets FEWER sections, never
  padded ones: "cut a section with no material" outranks the 4-7 range. Three strong
  sections plus the two closers beats seven thin ones.
- Each section opened by a bolded header with ONE emoji marker matched to
  content (📍 💰 🏗️ 📊 🏢 ⚡ 🗣️ 🏆 📈 📉 ✈️ 🏡). Emojis are section markers only,
  never decoration inside sentences.
- Facts as short lines, one per line. No numbered bullets, no dash bullets.
- One blank line between sections and between header and body.
- Include a short quote from a named source when the article has a strong one.
- Second-to-last section is always **💡 What this means for [market] Realtors**: the
  interpretation layer, and the most important section. One sharp insight beats many
  stats: what should an agent do, say, or watch because of this?
- Last section is always **✅ Bottom line**: recap + direct-address close. A pointed
  question works ("Are your buyers hearing this from you?").

## Article sourcing (in addition to the shared closed-world rule)

- Use ONLY facts, numbers, names, dates, and quotes from the provided article. Never
  round numbers into new claims.
- **Never state an inference as reported fact**, the most common X-reel failure:
  - Banned: "a county most agents have never worked" (nobody surveyed agents)
  - Banned: "builders can now put up buildings with zero parking" (minimums were
    eliminated; nobody said anyone will build zero)
  - Banned: "shortlist chatter usually runs months ahead" (general claim the source
    does not support)
  Cut the line or label it: "My take:" / "What I'd watch:".
- Prior context may be referenced only if the article or conversation establishes it.
- SEO terms woven naturally: market name, metro, Realtor, real estate market, mortgage
  when relevant.

---

## X-reel checklist (after the core self-check)

1. Hook label rotated vs. the prior post; bridge line varied and story-matched.
2. Hook leads with the most dramatic VERIFIED fact.
3. Caption under 1,800 characters including spaces and emojis, counted via the two-step
   method, CHARACTER count printed on the last line.
4. Every number and quote traceable to the article; zero unlabeled inferences.
5. "What this means for Realtors" delivers interpretation, not a stat recap.
6. Bottom line ends agent-facing: action, question, or challenge.
7. Market localized throughout, zero placeholders.
8. Bold only on section headers; no bullets-by-dash; blank lines between sections.
