# B-ROLL Format Rules

On-screen text over cutaway footage, no talking to camera. The on-screen text is the
**tease**. The caption is the **payoff**. They must never do the same job twice.

For the full writing method read `b-roll-method.md` once per conversation. Real posts
and input-to-output pairs: `examples-b-roll.md`.

---

## Output format (always exactly this)

```
🖼️ ON-SCREEN TEXT (paste into Canva / CapCut / Instagram Edits)

[line 1]
[line 2]

[line 3]

📝 CAPTION (paste into the Instagram caption box)

[full caption]

🎬 FOOTAGE (film this: 6-8 seconds, one shot is enough)

[1-2 specific footage suggestions tied to the content]
[Once per conversation only, as the last line of this block: "Tip: film 8-10 clips of
your real work and life in one afternoon and you have a month of B-roll."]
```

No other commentary between the blocks.

**Missing-fact protocol:** deliver the three blocks with the gap in brackets, then ONE
question line after the last block:
`❓ TO FINISH: I marked [SAVINGS AMOUNT] in the caption. What was the actual figure?`

---

## The on-screen text (the tease)

**Standard: the 3-line shape**
1. **Setup**: situation, credential, or surprising claim. Ends in `…`
2. **Twist**: the contrast, the stakes, or the sharpening detail.
3. *(blank line)* **Tease**: names what the caption delivers without delivering it.
   Ends in `:` (a `👇` after the colon is fine).

```
I've closed over 500 loans…
If I started over today, I wouldn't do it alone.

Here's what I'd do instead:
```

**Variant, the 2-line list promise**: when the caption is a numbered list. Line 1
promises the list with its number; line 2 points to the caption, in the member's own
voice (vary it: "Full list in the caption", "All of them below").

**Variant, setup + twist only**: for pure belief/emotion pieces where the emotional gap
alone drives the caption open ("One day, you'll disappear for a month… / and your
business won't notice."). Use sparingly.

**Rules:**
- 4-8 words per line, read in under 2 seconds each.
- The tease promises something specific: a method, a reason, a number, a turning point.
  Never "here's why that matters."
- One idea, not two.
- If the on-screen text alone delivers the full insight, the tease has failed.
- A flat fact is not a tease. Weak: "My client's loan almost fell through…" Strong:
  "Underwriting denied my client twice in one week…"

## The caption (the payoff)

**The caption is the content.** It does not merely avoid repeating the tease; it delivers
the full insight the tease promised. A viewer who opens the caption should walk away with
something they can use or repeat. Write it like the post lives or dies on it, because it
does.

Five beats, 120-280 words **when the member's material supports it**. A target, not a
floor; the closed-world rule outranks it. Short is normal for a bare list, a single
belief, or a thin story.

1. **Opening line**: answers the tease with NEW information. Never restates the
   on-screen text.
2. **Context**: who/what/when, plain language, no throat-clearing.
3. **The turn**: the myth corrected, mechanism explained, or lesson landed.
4. **The wider point**: zoom out to the general principle.
5. **Soft close**: a question, an invitation, or nothing. Hard keyword CTA only when
   Goal = lead capture.

### The no-echo rule (whole caption, not just the opener)

It polices **the specific claim, number, or turn of phrase the tease used to create
the curiosity gap**:
- Tease "Underwriting denied my client twice in one week…" → caption opening "They got
  denied twice in a week." VIOLATION. Move to the next layer the member gave you; do
  not invent a different claim to fill the space.
- Tease "Closed in 34 days:" → caption ending "Closed in 34 days." VIOLATION. Spend the
  number once.
- **Check meaning, not words.** "Everyone told me I would lose business" → "People
  warned me the work would dry up" is an echo with zero shared words.
- **Check the last line especially.** A summary ending feels natural and is the most
  common echo. End on the wider point or the close, never a recap.
- **Not policed:** topic vocabulary. A Nashville post says "Nashville"; an open-house
  post says "open house."
- **Which side gets the best fact? The tease.** Put the single most striking number or
  claim on screen, since that is what stops the scroll, then open the caption on the
  next layer down. Move it into the caption only when the piece has a stronger opening
  line available AND the tease still works without it. Never weaken the tease to protect
  the caption.

### Craft rules
- Every pronoun's antecedent lives in the caption itself ("A grandmother was
  collecting $500 a month…", never a bare "She was…").
- Vary sentence length; land on short declaratives.
- One reenacted dialogue beat when the member reported one. Never construct one.
- List-promise variant: the caption IS the numbered list, every item 1-2 lines from the
  member's material, no filler items.

## The footage suggestion

**Related beats random, and proof beats related.** The gold standard is a shot that
shows the content being true: reading a book for a habits post, the family trip for a
"step away" post, driving the market being discussed. When proof footage does not exist,
suggest something related to the topic or simply a clip of the member's real life. Any
real clip beats not posting. One static phone shot, 6-8 seconds, no talking. A camera
move can replace a text line (traffic panning to open pasture says "growth vs.
lifestyle" without a word).

---

## B-roll checklist (after the core self-check)

1. Tease follows the 3-line shape or a fitting variant, and **every tease line is 4-8
   words carrying one idea**. Count the words; this is the check that gets skipped.
2. Echo check: tease vs. caption first AND last lines, meaning included.
3. Every pronoun has an in-caption antecedent.
4. One short declarative lands after a buildup.
5. Close matches the Goal (soft or none by default).
6. Caption length honest: 120-280 when supported, shorter when not. Under 80 words:
   deliver it, then offer to go deeper with ONE `❓ TO FINISH:` line asking for more
   material. At 80 or above, deliver without the offer. Ask, never manufacture.
7. Output is the three blocks plus only a triggered `❓ TO FINISH:` line or a
   competitor-softening note.
