## Example 1: Corporate relocation story

**Source article summary:** A Fortune 500 electronics company announced it is moving its
U.S. headquarters to a suburb of the loan officer's metro, relocating 1,000 employees by
year end. The suburb already hosts several major corporate campuses.

**Output:**

JUST IN: [Company] is moving its U.S. headquarters to [Suburb].

1,000 employees relocating to the [Metro] area by end of year.

Here's who's coming to town 👇

---

**CAPTION:**

**📦 The move:**

U.S. HQ relocating from [Old State] to [Suburb]
1,000 employees moving by December
Employees notified last week
Exact location not yet disclosed

**🏢 The company's size:**

Fortune 500 ranked No. 27
$224 billion in revenue
30-year presence in the state already

**📍 [Suburb]'s corporate roster:**

[Anchor company]: New campus bringing 10,000 jobs
[Second company]: Already headquartered here
[Third and fourth companies]: Major campuses

**🏙️ The trajectory:**

One of the metro's fastest-growing corporate destination cities
Strong talent pool, highly rated schools, highway access
Increasingly a destination city, not just a suburb

**💡 What this means for [Metro] Realtors:**

1,000 employees need housing by December
These are high-income workers with serious buying power
[Suburb] and surrounding cities are in the bull's-eye for relocation buyers

Agents working this corridor are sitting on one of the hottest relocation markets in the country.

**✅ Bottom line:**

[Company]'s U.S. HQ is leaving [Old State] and landing in [Suburb].

Corporate relocations mean relocation buyers. Are you positioned to capture them?

---

**Character count: [~1,450]**

**Why this works:**
- Hook uses JUST IN for a fresh announcement and leads with the biggest fact (HQ move + employee count)
- Sections move from news facts → local context → interpretation
- The Realtor section converts the news into an agent opportunity, not a stat recap
- Bottom line ends on a direct challenge question

## Example 2: Safety ranking / data story

**Source article summary:** A national study ranked a suburb in the loan officer's market
the No. 2 safest big city in the U.S., while the metro's core city landed among the least
safe in the same study.

**Output:**

REPORT: [Suburb] just ranked the No. 2 safest big city in America.

[Core City] landed on the least safe list in the same study.

Here's how the study breaks down 👇

---

**CAPTION:**

**🏆 The ranking:**

[Suburb]: No. 2 safest big city in the U.S.
Only [Top City] ranked higher
Study evaluated 83 U.S. cities with populations of 250,000+

**📊 What the study measured:**

Traffic fatalities
Natural disaster risk
Property crime
Violent crime risk

Data sourced from the FBI, FEMA and the CDC.

**📉 [Suburb]'s numbers:**

Among the nation's lowest violent crime rates
Among the nation's lowest property crime rates
It happens year after year.

**💡 What this means for [Metro] Realtors:**

Safety is one of the top three questions relocation buyers ask
[Suburb] now has data-backed proof to answer any hesitation
The contrast with [Core City] gives suburban agents an even stronger story

Pair this ranking with everything else happening in [Suburb] right now. This is a city building a reputation.

**✅ Bottom line:**

[Suburb] ranked second safest big city in America while [Core City] landed on the least safe list.

If your buyers are weighing suburbs, safety data just became one of your strongest closing tools. Use it.

---

**Character count: [~1,350]**

**Why this works:**
- Hook uses REPORT for a data/ranking story and contrast (safest vs. least safe) for tension
- Neutral, fact-based handling of a potentially sensitive comparison
- Realtor section names the exact conversation agents can have with buyers
- Closes with a direct instruction: "Use it."

## Common failure modes to avoid

1. **Running long.** The CAPTION section creeping past 2,000 characters is the
   most common failure. Cut whole facts, keep line breaks.
2. **Stat dumps.** Listing every number from the article. Pick the ones that build the
   story; the Realtor section should interpret, not recap.
3. **Buried hook.** Opening with background instead of the most dramatic fact.
4. **Generic takeaways.** "This is great news for the market!" is worthless. Name what an
   agent should do, say, or watch.
5. **Invented context.** Adding projections, comparisons, or "experts say" claims not in
   the article.
6. **Losing the market.** Forgetting to localize SEO terms and the Realtor section to the
   loan officer's actual metro.
