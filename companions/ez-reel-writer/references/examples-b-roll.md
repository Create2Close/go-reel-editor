# B-Roll Examples: Real Posts, Real Numbers

> **How to use these examples:** they show structure and the quality bar. SKILL.md's
> rules outrank any wording modeled here; if an example ever demonstrates something the
> rules ban, the rules win. Never reuse these examples' prose in a member's post.
> Computed tables are the exception: correct arithmetic looks the same everywhere.

Four real B-roll reels analyzed frame-by-frame (real estate / mortgage creators).
Study the pattern each one uses before writing.

---

## Example 1: Textbook 3-line tease (Steve Beskid, GO coach)

```
ON-SCREEN TEXT (staggered ~2.5s apart):
I only have one regret in business:
I was a terrible leader

Here's what I got wrong👇
```

- Footage: Steve in a leather armchair, fidgeting, not talking. 7.5s.
- Caption opens with NEW info: "Before I started my own company…" → vulnerable story
  about failing his assistant. Soft close.
- Result: small reach but the highest comment rate of the analyzed set (0.30%) and a
  1.21% like rate. Vulnerability converts.
- Lesson: the twist line ("I was a terrible leader") gets the boldest visual treatment.

## Example 2: 2-line list promise (Amir Syed, GO coach), 56.6K plays

```
ON-SCREEN TEXT:
21 habits to be incredible at sales:        [static from 0:00]
Read the caption below ⬇️                    [fades in ~0:03.5]
```

- Footage: Amir reading a physical book: which IS habit #2 on his list. Proof, not
  wallpaper.
- Caption: all 21 habits, numbered, each 1–2 lines, concrete ("Role play every single
  week", "Record your loan consultations… watch them back like game tape").
- Result: 1.59% like rate: belief/value content earns approval.
- Lesson: big odd number ("21") signals depth; on-theme footage sells authenticity.

## Example 3: Setup + twist, no tease line (Amir Syed, GO coach)

```
ON-SCREEN TEXT (static, two lines):
One day, you'll disappear for a month…
and your business won't notice.
```

- Footage: his wife and daughter walking through a Lake Como resort, the footage IS
  the disappearance. 5.5s.
- Caption opens: "That's the month you should be building toward." (new info, zero
  echo). Pure worldview. **No CTA at all.**
- Result: best like rate (1.96%) AND best comment rate of the whole B-roll set.
- Lesson: on deeply personal belief content, the emotional gap replaces the tease line,
  and no CTA outperforms any CTA.

## Example 4: List promise + hard keyword CTA (Kari Cummings), 53.8K plays

```
ON-SCREEN TEXT (static, one block):
When people move to Dallas, TX only to regret
it and move away 2 years later… it's usually
because of these 4 things
```

- Footage: drive-by shot of the Dallas skyline, location proof.
- Caption: the 4 numbered reasons + hard CTA "Comment GUIDE" for a relocation guide.
- Result: 17x plays-per-view (text deliberately too long to read in one pass = loop
  bait) and a lead-capture engine.
- Lesson: the hard-CTA build trades like-rate for reach and leads. Use when the user
  says lead capture is the goal.

## Advanced variant (reference only: don't default to it)

Jimmy Conover's 4.83M-play reel: one 27-second sentence split across 11 sequential
lower-third text lines, each completing a clause over a matching family-footage clip
("my wife told me she wanted to leave Arizona… / we could have 4 seasons, / live a
slower pace of life, / …"). Viewers can't exit mid-sentence. 4.96% like rate, growth
via shares. Same writing skill (one good sentence), 11 clips instead of 1. Offer this
shape only when the user has abundant personal footage and an emotional story.

# Member input to finished output: two real pairs

These show the target quality from real (tested) member inputs. Study the moves, never
reuse the sentences.

## Pair 1: a rambling voice note becomes a clean tease + payoff

**Member sent:**
> "Make me a b-roll, I'm just gonna talk this out. so today was
> insane, I had three showings and the second one the couple was super nice, they've
> been looking since like March I think? maybe April. anyway they keep losing out on
> houses and I realized after this one that it's because they keep waiting for the
> weekend to go see stuff and by then it's gone. like the house today had been on 4
> days and had 6 offers. and they were like well we can only look Saturdays. and I said
> that's the whole problem. anyway also my car needs an oil change lol. but yeah the
> ones who win are the ones who can go Tuesday at 2pm. that's it. that's the whole
> thing."

**Delivered on-screen text:**
> A house on the market 4 days…
> It had 6 offers.
>
> Here's what decides who gets it:

**Delivered caption:**
> My second showing today was with a couple who have been looking since the spring.
> Nice people. They keep losing out on houses, and after this one I saw why.
>
> They wait for the weekend.
>
> They told me they can only look Saturdays. I said that was the whole problem. By the
> time Saturday comes, the house they wanted to see is gone.
>
> The ones who win are the ones who can go Tuesday at 2pm. That's it.
>
> If you're house hunting, take a hard look at your Tuesdays.

**Why this is the bar:** every claim traces to the member's words. The hedge "March I
think? maybe April" became "since the spring" (not "since March"). "They were like"
became plain reported speech with nothing built around it. The oil-change tangent
vanished. The Tuesday claim stayed general, no "in Boise right now". The tease spends
the 4-days/6-offers numbers and the caption never repeats them.

## Pair 2: a missing number gets bracketed, never invented

**Member sent (excerpt):**
> "people move to Nashville expecting it to be cheap and then get blindsided, property
> taxes are low but insurance has gone way up, traffic on 65 is brutal…"

**Delivered caption (excerpt):**
> The property tax bill really is low. That part of the reputation holds up.
>
> Then the insurance quote lands. Premiums here have gone up [INSURANCE INCREASE].
> What I tell clients: both of those show up on the same monthly payment, so the tax
> number on its own will not tell you what a house costs you here.

**Plus one question after the blocks:**
> ❓ TO FINISH: I marked [INSURANCE INCREASE] in the caption. How much have premiums
> actually gone up, and over what period?

**Why this is the bar:** the member said "way up" with no figure. The output neither
invented a number nor hid behind "a good chunk". It bracketed the gap, delivered a
complete usable draft, and asked exactly one question. The causal link the member
implied got labeled "What I tell clients:", a take, never a reported fact.
